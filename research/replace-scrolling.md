## 1. Landscape: apps that interrupt or replace scrolling

### 1.1 Friction / deliberation blockers (intervene between intention and app)

The defining finding for this whole category: in the peer-reviewed field study of [one sec](https://one-sec.app/) (Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, *PNAS* 2023), adding a deliberate interruption before a "target app" opens — a 10-second delay, a breathing animation, and an explicit option to dismiss — reduced users' actual openings of target apps by **57%** over six weeks (280 participants): **36% of attempts were dismissed at the interception**, and total opening *attempts* fell **37%** vs. week one ([PNAS 120(8):e2213114120](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/)). Mechanism matters: in the preregistered online experiment (N=500), **the option to dismiss was the single most effective feature**; friction/delay also worked; the deliberation *message alone did nothing* (same paper). The app's author is a co-author of the study (disclosed), and the authors flag self-selection: users who install one sec are not representative of all social-media users ([PNAS paper, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/)) — so treat "57%" as *efficacy in a motivated self-selected population*, not a market-wide average.

### 1.3 Snackable-knowledge apps (closest competitors)

These don't block anything; they compete for the same "reach for phone, get dopamine" slot with content instead of feeds.

- **DeepStash** — positions itself explicitly against doomscrolling: homepage tagline "Replace doomscrolling with microlearning," and marketing copy includes literally **"Become the 'interesting person'"** and "Dominate conversations" ([deepstash.com](https://deepstash.com/)) — the exact "most interesting person in the room" positioning this project intends to counter, from a competitor. Claimed 3M+ users, 10M+ Play downloads (self-reported, same site). Swiping through idea cards is structurally *still a feed* — the anti-doomscroll positioning coexists with infinite-scroll UI.
- **Blinkist** — 15-min nonfiction summaries + audio; yearly plans reported ~$99.99–174.99 depending on source/plan ([nibble price guide](https://nibble-app.com/blog/blinkist-cost); [booksummaryclub comparison](https://booksummaryclub.com/blinkist-vs-headway/)) **[pricing varies by source/region — [UNVERIFIED] exact current numbers]**. Acquires users via paid social (Facebook Ads, Taboola) ([integrate.io case study](https://www.integrate.io/blog/data-products-how-blinkist-drives-engagement-and-retention-through-personalization/)).
- **Headway** — microlearning "key ideas," gamified progress, "Tired of mindless doom-scrolling?" is literally its Google Play listing hook ([Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.headway.books&hl=en_US)); reported ~$89.99/yr ([makeheadway cost comparison](https://makeheadway.com/blog/cost-comparison-of-book-summary-apps-worldwide/)) **[vendor-adjacent source]**.
- **Shortform** — deeper interactive book guides; $197/yr reported ([littlealmanack review](https://www.littlealmanack.com/p/shortform-vs-headway-vs-blinkist)).
- **Elevate** — daily brain-training games for reading/writing/math; Apple's 2014 iPhone App of the Year ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/elevate-brain-training-games/id875063456); [Mind Company blog](https://themindcompany.com/blog/elevate-apple-app-of-the-year)). Its "designed with neuroscience experts" framing is **marketing**; brain-training transfer evidence is contested (see §3).
- **Brilliant** — interactive STEM problem-solving courses, subscription ([brilliant.org](https://brilliant.org/)); **[UNVERIFIED]** specifics this session.
- **Curiosity** — daily short knowledge explainers ([curiosity.com](https://curiosity.com/)); **[UNVERIFIED]** specifics this session.
- **Quibi (failure case)** — raised ~$1.75B for premium short-form mobile video, launched April 2020, shut down October 2020 after ~6 months ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/21/business/media/quibi-shutting-down.html); [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/media/quibi-weighs-shutting-down-as-problems-mount-11603301946)). Lesson relevant to mediaReplacement: expensive short-form *content* lost to free user-generated short-form; a "premium version of the feed" is not a defensible replacement — the habit slot was already taken.
- **Duolingo** — the retention machine of the category. Streak mechanics + loss aversion (Streak Society, streak freezes) drive daily return; record 17M DAU mid-2023 credited largely to streaks ([businessofapps](https://www.businessofapps.com/news/duolingos-streak-feature-drives-record-17-million-daus/)); reported 58.7M DAU in Q2 2026 with record ~84% retention ([pulse2 citing earnings](https://pulse2.com/duolingo-daily-active-users-reach-58-7-million-as-retention-hits-record-84-and-social-accounts-top-1-billion-organic-impressions/)) and >10M users holding 365-day streaks ([games.gg report](https://games.gg/news/duolingo-hits-10-million-users-with-365-day-login-streak/)) **[secondary aggregators of IR data — treat as directional]**. WSJ profiled it as "the only app that always wins the battle for your attention" ([WSJ, 2024](https://www.wsj.com/tech/personal-tech/duolingo-streaks-notifications-app-spanish-bc87d6e4)). Free tier is ad-supported; monetization is subscription (Super) — gamification serves retention, which serves conversion.
- **Daily philosophy** — [Daily Stoic](https://dailystoic.com/) (free daily email + app of quotes/texts); [Stoa](https://stoameditation.com/) (Stoic texts + meditation, subscription, [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daily-stoic-philosophy-stoa/id1446345567)). Prove there is an existing audience for a "philosophy snack" slot, but they are quote-of-the-day apps, not deep engagement engines.

**Gap the landscape leaves open:** blockers *subtract* (friction, blocking) and snackable apps *substitute* (feeds of summaries) — but none of the surveyed apps combines deliberate friction at the scroll-entry point with **an actual learning loop that pays the user back** (progress you can verify, review you actually retain). DeepStash even shows the substitute-feed model drifting back toward the feed mechanics it claims to oppose.

## 2. What works: the science

### 2.1 Attention

- **Gloria Mark (UC Irvine), 15+ years of screen-attention logging:** average time on any one screen before self-interruption fell from ~2.5 min (2004) to ~75 s (2012) to **~47 seconds** in her recent studies; refocusing after an interruption takes **~25 minutes** — her book *Attention Span* (2023) popularized these longitudinal results ([gloriamark.com](https://gloriamark.com/attention-span/); [APA Speaking of Psychology interview](https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/attention-spans); [transcript interview](https://www.steelcase.com/research/articles/our-47-second-attention-span-with-gloria-mark-s5-ep3-transcript/)). Design consequence: a replacement product is fighting a trained ~47-second attention rhythm; the *first* design requirement is expanding the dwell-time budget, not adding more snackable content.
- **Flow (Csikszentmihalyi):** deep engagement requires clear goals, immediate feedback, and challenge–skill balance; attention fully invested in an activity is the subjective hallmark of optimal experience (foundational: *Flow*, 1990; contemporary reviews: [scoping review of flow in environments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S027249442500088X); [conceptual integration of flow + attention restoration](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11050943/)). Flow states are the *opposite* of the 47-second shuffle — and are achievable in reading and problem-solving, not in swiping cards.
- **Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan & Kaplan 1989; Kaplan 1995):** directed attention fatigues; restorative environments (soft fascination, being-away, extent, compatibility) rebuild it ([USDA symposium paper](https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs/rmrs_p064/rmrs_p064_140_146.pdf); [SAGE qualitative study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2055102916637090)). Implication: the app should not consume all attention; designing in breaks/softness (or non-screen interludes) is theory-aligned.

### 2.2 Smartphone habit formation and "addiction"

- **Problematic smartphone use (PSU):** the literature converges on real, measurable harms at the excessive end but **contests "addiction" framing** — smartphone addiction is not a DSM-5 diagnosis ([overview of terminology](https://www.rewirelabs.app/phone-addiction-symptoms)); associations with well-being are better described as inverse-U-shaped, with *excessive* use driving harm, per the widely cited Przybylski reconciliation — see discussion with citations in the [one sec PNAS paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/). Consensus statements flag attention problems and behavioural-addiction correlations in adolescents ([consensus statement, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391773560_A_consensus_statement_on_potential_negative_impacts_of_smartphone_and_social_media_use_on_adolescent_mental_health)).
- **Variable-ratio reinforcement / slot-machine design:** the engagement mechanics of feeds (intermittent reward schedules) are standard behavioral-psychology fare; the "pull-to-refresh as slot-machine lever" framing originates in design commentary (e.g., Lorenz-Spreen and colleagues on the attention economy, and 60 Minutes' 2017 "Brain Hacking" reporting) — **[UNVERIFIED in primary academic form this session]**; treat as mechanism-plausible rather than proven. Anna Lembke's *Dopamine Nation* (2021) frames the pleasure–pain balance: high-reward, low-effort stimulation shifts baseline and drives compulsive loop-seeking ([annalembke.com](https://www.annalembke.com/dopamine-nation); [NPR interview](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1030930259?ft=nprml&f=139790396)) — a clinician's popular synthesis, influential but **a book, not trial evidence**.
- **What actually reduces problematic use — RCT/intervention evidence:**
  - **Reduced (not eliminated) use beats abstinence framing:** one sec's authors note reduced-use interventions show better well-being outcomes than total-abstinence interventions ([PNAS paper, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/)).
  - **Screen-time reduction RCT (2025, BMC Medicine):** 3-week smartphone screen-time reduction showed small-to-medium effects on depression, stress, sleep, well-being ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39985031/); [BMC Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-025-03944-z)).
  - **Nudge-based smartphone intervention:** 2-week smartphone nudge (self-monitoring prompts) reduced problematic use, screen time, and depressive symptoms across two studies ([PMC9112639](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9112639/)).
  - **Social-media reduction RCTs** (Brailovskaia et al., reducing Facebook use → life satisfaction ↑, depressive symptoms ↓) are summarized in the one sec paper's references ([PNAS paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/)).
  - **Digital minimalism interventions** (Cal Newport, *Digital Minimalism*, 2019; Catherine Price, *How to Break Up with Your Phone*, 2018) are practitioner frameworks with large followings; **no RCT support of the books' programs themselves [UNVERIFIED / not found]** — their prescriptions overlap with what RCTs do support (friction, notifications off, scheduled use), which is the defensible way to cite them.
- **Where evidence is weak/contested:** long-term effects of friction (the PNAS study is 6 weeks); generalization beyond self-selected motivated users; effect sizes for "digital detox" studies are generally small-to-medium and short-lived ([Georgetown summary of detox meta-research](https://www.georgetown.edu/news/digital-detox-reduce-screen-time-benefits/)).

### 2.3 Mindfulness trials (for the "replace with Calm" temptation)

Headspace/Calm have published-ish trial support for app-delivered mindfulness reducing stress/anxiety in some populations, but the flagship consumer apps' own marketing efficacy claims are not uniformly trial-backed ([University of Melbourne review of mental-health app "science"](https://www.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/4824404/Mental-Health-Apps-Report-updated-17-Dec.pdf)) **[UNVERIFIED specifics this session]**. Don't over-index on meditation as the replacement content; it treats the symptom (stress) not the substitution (boredom loop).

## 3. Learning-format evidence — what survives contact with users

### 3.1 Progress tracking + reviews (owner requirement: real value or it's useless)

- **Khan Academy mastery system** — the canonical progress-tracking model: skills move through **Familiar → Proficient → Mastered** as you practice, and **Mastery Challenges** surface previously-learned skills for spaced review, leveling them up or down on performance ([What are Course and Unit Mastery?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery); [Mastery Challenges in course mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037127892-What-are-Mastery-Challenges-in-course-mastery)). Note for designers: Khan explicitly decays mastery (skills can drop if challenges go badly) — progress is *measured*, not *granted* ([How mastery levels work](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)).
- **Duolingo** — XP measures effort, not mastery (community-documented: [Duolingo Wiki, XP](https://duolingo.fandom.com/wiki/XP) — a level is "how much work you have put in," **not** "your level of mastery" [secondary wiki]); Crowns/levels sequence skills, and the 2022 path redesign unified crowns and path steps ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)). The streak system (§1.3) is the retention layer. Lesson: engagement metrics (XP/streaks) and mastery metrics (crowns, later path review circles) are **separate systems that Duolingo deliberately keeps distinct**.
- **Exercism** — language **tracks** organized by concepts with exercises + automated analysis + human mentoring, entirely free ([exercism.org](https://exercism.org/); [track example, Python: 146 exercises in 17 concepts](https://exercism.org/tracks/python)). Proof that a "practice-first, no-feed" learning surface can retain without gamified streaks dominating.
- **The evidence underneath all three — review-based retention:**
  - **Distributed (spaced) practice:** meta-analysis of 31 effects (N>3000) in classroom settings found a moderate effect favoring distributed over massed practice, **d = 0.54** ([Latimier-style meta-review, PMC12189222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12189222/)); the classic Cepeda et al. (2006) meta-analysis established spacing as one of the most powerful memory effects ([Cepeda 2006 PDF](https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf)).
  - **Retrieval practice / active recall:** massed learning can look better immediately but decays within a week, while spaced retrieval retains ([Frontiers experiment](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.685245/full)); a physiology-course study found a retrieval+spacing strategy improved exam performance by ~16% ([Advances in Physiology Education](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00173.2025)).
  - **Progress monitoring:** meta-analysis of the experimental evidence (Harkin et al., *Psychological Bulletin* 2016) found monitoring goal progress reliably promotes goal attainment (**DOI: 10.1037/bul0000025**, verified via Crossref). Progress *display* is an intervention in itself — which directly supports the owner's requirement that the app must track and show real progress, not just content.

### 3.3 Microlearning efficacy — read the fine print

- Vendor framing (Headway, Blinkist, DeepStash) sells microlearning as a *replacement for reading*, but the peer-reviewed picture is narrower: systematic reviews of microlearning (higher-ed meta-analysis showing positive effects on retention/outcomes; [ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844024174440); [2025 meta-analysis](https://publikasi.teknokrat.ac.id/index.php/jurnalmathema/article/view/517)) also stress **high heterogeneity of designs and measures** ([OSF higher-ed review](https://osf.io/download/fhu8n/)) — i.e., effects are real but not comparable across contexts, and no major book-summary vendor publishes outcome trials. Treat "learn in 10 minutes/day" as **marketing** unless a cited study exists.
- **Book summaries ≠ the book:** even vendor-adjacent reviews concede summaries can't replace the deep narrative of a 400-page book ([Headway's own review roundup of Reddit sentiment](https://makeheadway.com/blog/headway-app-reddit-reviews/)). The classics/philosophy positioning of mediaReplacement is *differentiated* against this: summaries skim the surface that mediaReplacement intends to drill into.
- **Brain-training caution:** Lumosity's $2M FTC settlement (2016) for unsupported cognition claims is the category's cautionary precedent ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pays-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-bright)) — Elevate/Brilliant-style "backed by science" claims should be treated as advertising until trials are cited.

### 3.4 Streaks, gamification, long-form, explainers, communication practice

- **Gamification effect sizes:** meta-analyses find significant but **small positive effects** of gamification on cognitive, motivational, and behavioral learning outcomes ([PMC8037535](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8037535/)); Duolingo's streak data (§1.3) is the strongest *commercial* demonstration that streak mechanics retain users daily — but retention ≠ fluency gain.
- **Long-form reading of classics:** print/long-form supports comprehension better than screen reading for many measures, though the digital-reading deficit reverses with age/plain-text conditions — evidence is medium-dependent, not absolute ([ScienceDirect review citing Delgado et al. 2018, Kong et al. 2018, Singer & Alexander 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883035522001136); [on-task attention study, screen vs print](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7463273/); [no-negative-effects-when-plain-text study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15213269.2022.2070216)). Design takeaway: long chapters of Socrates/Plato *can* work on a phone if the reading surface is deliberately distraction-free (plain text, no feed adjacency).
- **Explainer content ("how cars work"):** the Animagraffs tradition — Jake O'Neal's animated/3D infographics explaining machines (cars, fire engines, NASCAR) — descends from Stephen Biesty-style cross-section books ([animagraffs.com](https://animagraffs.com/); [about page: "I grew up taking things apart... cross-section books like Stephen Biesty's"](https://animagraffs.com/about/)). This is a *proven engagement format* (millions of views, long-form infographics that hold attention for minutes-to-tens-of-minutes) with no app of its own — a real gap. No outcome-evidence exists that explainer content builds transferable knowledge (it's engagement-first content) — treat as "better than the feed," not "as good as study."
- **Communication-skills practice:** the Toastmasters model (structured practice + immediate evaluative feedback + repetition) is the de-facto standard for deliberate public-speaking practice ([Toastmasters program described in experiential-learning research](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375237802_Experiential_Learning_and_Toastmasters_International_Programme_Transformative_Effects_of_Public_Speaking_Training_on_Oral_Presentations)); deliberate-practice research (Ericsson tradition) underlies the "practice a specific sub-skill, get feedback, repeat" design. Direct RCTs of *app-based* communication-skill drills: none found **[honest gap]**.
- **Spaced repetition / SRS for vocabulary-style content:** the strongest-evidence learning mechanic in this entire document (§3.1) and the one no incumbent blocker or summary app fully implements — a genuine differentiator for a "replacement feed."

### 3.5 The "1% better each day" framing — rhetoric vs. evidence

- **What it claims:** James Clear's *Atomic Habits* framing: improving 1% every day compounds — "if you master continuous improvement and get 1% better each day for one year, you'll end up 37 times better" (1.01^365 ≈ 37.8) ([jamesclear.com/continuous-improvement](https://jamesclear.com/continuous-improvement); the 37× figure is his site's own headline math). It is motivational framing built from compounding *arithmetic*, not from a measured outcome.
- **What the math actually is:** the claim assumes a constant, perfectly-compounding improvement rate in a skill; real learning is not multiplicative in that way (skills plateau, decay, and reorganize). No study cited by the source demonstrates a 37× skill gain; it's a **motivational heuristic about small-daily-actions**, not an effect size. Mark it as rhetoric-with-a-true-spirit.
- **What does have evidence nearby:** (a) **progress monitoring** — meta-analysis of experimental evidence found monitoring goal progress promotes goal attainment (Harkin et al., *Psychological Bulletin* 2016, DOI 10.1037/bul0000025, verified via Crossref) — i.e., *tracking* has measurable effect, but modestly and on attainment of defined goals, not via magic compounding; (b) **spacing** — small, repeated, distributed sessions beat massed cramming (d = 0.54, §3.1) — the direction (small frequent reps beat rare big ones) is real even though the "37×" magnitude is not; (c) **spaced review of declining skills** (Khan's mastery decay, §3.1) is the honest implementation of the same intuition.
- **Design translation:** a daily micro-improvement *frame* is fine for motivation, but the product should track and display **mastery-retention curves** (what you still know over time), not a compounding "level" that implies the 37× math. Don't put unverified compounding claims in marketing copy (same standard as §3.3's brain-training caution).

## 4. The ad-funded incumbents and privacy

### 4.1 The regulatory pattern proof: BetterHelp

This is the pattern proof: **a subscription wellness product can still monetize user data to ad platforms.**

### 4.3 What "privacy-focused" means technically in this product category

1. **No third-party ad/attribution/analytics SDKs** — the single differentiator the Exodus census shows is feasible: the category's incumbents ship 6–8 trackers; a privacy-first build ships zero (compare: FOSS Android apps on the [F-Droid repository](https://f-droid.org/) are a community standard for no-tracking distributions; example: **Mindful** self-labels "(OpenSource)" on its [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mindful.android&hl=en_US)).
2. **Local-first data:** progress, streaks, and library state in an on-device store; sync optional and encrypted. There is no technical reason a spaced-repetition scheduler can't run entirely on-device (the algorithms are public — §3.1) **[INFERENCE from algorithmic literature]**.
3. **Minimal permissions:** the incumbents' Android packages request 15–23 permissions including advertising services; a reading/review app needs a fraction of that (Exodus permission counts cited above per app).
4. **No account required** (or local-only account): removes the analytics-identity joining key and most of the compliance surface.
5. **Monetization without ads:** one-time purchase / subscription / donations — each incumbent already demonstrates subscription viability at $70–100/yr (Headspace, Calm, Blinkist, Headway cited above), and blocker apps show freemium friction models work (one sec, Opal) and donation-funding works (ScreenZen). The incentive argument: ad-funding optimizes for time-on-app (the addiction dynamic the whole category is fighting); paid models optimize for outcome-delivery.

## 5. Platform constraints (engineering reality check)

### iOS — Screen Time API (FamilyControls / ManagedSettings / DeviceActivity)

- Apple ships a first-party Screen Time API stack: **FamilyControls** (authorization + opaque app tokens), **ManagedSettings** (shields/blocking), **DeviceActivity** (monitoring + Device Activity Monitor extension) — introduced WWDC 2021 with a privacy-first framing ([Meet the Screen Time API, WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10123/); [Screen Time Technology Frameworks index](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/screentimeapidocumentation)).
- **Entitlement process:** distributing an app (or any Screen Time API app extension — Device Activity Monitor, Shield Action/Configuration) that uses FamilyControls requires the **Family Controls (Distribution) entitlement**, requested by the Apple Developer Account Holder from Apple and subject to Apple review/approval before it's added as a managed capability ([Requesting the Family Controls entitlement — Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols/requesting-the-family-controls-entitlement)).
- Practical limits (community-documented): tokens are opaque (the app learns *categories*, not app identities, from the API's perspective), monitoring granularity and shield UX are constrained, and approval timelines are opaque **[UNVERIFIED — developer-forum-reported]**.

### Android — UsageStatsManager, Accessibility, and Play policy

- **UsageStatsManager** (`android.app.usage`) provides app-usage statistics and foreground detection, gated behind a special user-granted "Usage access" permission ([Android API reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/accessibilityservice/AccessibilityService); [developer request for better Digital Wellbeing interoperability, noting the gap, Google IssueTracker #454083282](https://issuetracker.google.com/issues/454083282)).
- **Accessibility-based blocking is policy-constrained:** Google Play's [Use of the AccessibilityService API policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en) permits the API across a wide range of apps but **prohibits any use that lets an app autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions/decisions**, and restricts it for non-accessibility purposes ([Permissions and APIs policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en)) — i.e., blocker apps using AccessibilityService live on a policy knife-edge and have faced enforcement; Android 13 additionally restricts enabling accessibility services for sideloaded apps ([Esper analysis](https://esper.io/blog/android-13-sideloading-restriction-harder-malware-abuse-accessibility-apis)). Play Protect can flag non-conforming apps ([developer guidance](https://developers.google.com/android/play-protect/warning-dev-guidance)).
- Device-owner/DevicePolicyManager routes exist but are impractical for a consumer app ([DevicePolicyManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager.html)).

**Net:** the honest engineering baseline is *iOS: first-party, entitlement-gated, strongest; Android: UsageStats + overlay/dialog techniques, Accessibility only if the policy position is explicitly accepted* — which is exactly the constraint every blocker in §1.1 already navigates.

## 6. Design implications for mediaReplacement

Derived strictly from the evidence above; each principle cites its source section.

1. **Open with friction, not content.** The strongest causal evidence in this document is the one sec study: an explicit *option to dismiss* at the moment of app-opening cuts openings by 57% (6 weeks, N=280), with delay helpful and bare messaging useless (§1.1, [PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/)). mediaReplacement should intercept the scroll-entry moment first — then offer the replacement.
2. **The replacement must pay back, measurably.** The owner's bar (§3.1): mastery-style progress with decay (Khan's Familiar/Proficient/Mastered + Mastery Challenges) plus spaced repetition/active recall — the only learning mechanic here with meta-analytic backing (d = 0.54 spacing; retrieval-practice advantages; Harkin monitoring effect, DOI 10.1037/bul0000025) (§3.1). A feed of interesting snippets with no verification would repeat DeepStash's trap: anti-doomscroll marketing over feed mechanics (§1.3).
3. **Keep streaks, but demote them.** Streak mechanics demonstrably drive daily return (Duolingo, §1.3) — use them for *habit formation* (return + review), but keep XP/engagement metrics distinct from mastery metrics the way Duolingo separates crowns from XP (§3.1). Beware slot-machine mechanics: variable-ratio reward loops are the incumbent's engagement engine; a replacement product should reward *completing sessions*, not opening sessions (§2.2).
4. **Dwell-time budget as the core design metric.** Users' trained attention window is ~47 s (§2.1). Default session shapes should start short (one micro-lesson or one spaced-review card set) and grow via flow-compatible challenge (clear goals, immediate feedback — §2.1), not via autoplayed adjacency.
5. **Hand off to the best tool; don't duplicate it.** Where stable public surfaces exist (Khan course deep links, Exercism tracks + CLI flow, OCW course pages — §3.2), link out; build no shallow clone of what those tools already do better. Never build on unofficial third-party APIs or credential-sharing (Duolingo cautionary case, §3.2).
6. **No ad/attribution SDKs, ever; minimal permissions; local-first.** The regulatory pattern (BetterHelp, §4.1) and the tracker census (§4.2) show subscription wellness apps monetizing identity anyway; the category's privacy preconditions are: zero third-party trackers, no account (or local-only), on-device data (§4.3). Monetize via subscription/one-time/patron — every incumbent proves willingness-to-pay in this range (§1.3, §4.3), and ScreenZen proves donation-funded is survivable (§1.1).
7. **Long-form and explainer content works *if* the surface is quiet.** Print-quality long-form reading survives on mobile under plain-text, distraction-free conditions (§3.4), and the Animagraffs/Biesty cross-section tradition is a proven attention-holding format with no dedicated app (§3.4). Build the classics/philosophy reading surface as deliberate *deep* reading (no feed adjacency), and "how things work" as a curated explainer shelf — not as cards.
8. **Practice, don't preach, for communication skills.** The evidence-backed communication format is structured practice + immediate feedback + repetition (Toastmasters/deliberate practice, §3.4) — e.g., record-and-reflect drills or real-conversation checklists, not more reading about communication.
9. **Be honest about what the product cannot claim.** Effectiveness claims in this category are marketing until trialed: Lumosity paid $2M for unsupported cognition claims (§3.3), microlearning vendors publish no outcome trials (§3.3), and the strongest intervention evidence in the space is "reduced use improves well-being" in motivated users over weeks, not months (§2.2). Copy should claim what the RCT-level evidence supports: friction reduces openings; review-based progress improves retention; the rest is hypothesis.
10. **Watch the drift back toward the feed.** DeepStash demonstrates that an anti-doomscroll app can grow into a doomscroll-shaped product (infinite swipe feed + status messaging, §1.3). Design review should include a standing question: *does any surface here replicate the variable-ratio mechanics we're replacing?* (§2.2).

## Open questions

1. **Retention numbers are private.** None of the friction/learning apps publishes cohort retention or long-term outcome data; category comparisons rely on reviews and anecdote. Any mediaReplacement retention target is a guess until measured.
2. **Longevity of friction effects.** The one sec study covers 6 weeks in self-selected users; decay curves beyond that are unknown (the authors say so themselves, §1.1).
3. **Apple Family Controls entitlement approval** — how long and how selective for a new indie developer is community-documented only **[UNVERIFIED]**; it materially affects the iOS roadmap.
4. **Android enforcement climate for blocker apps** — the policy text is clear; actual Play enforcement patterns (how often blocker apps lose Accessibility approval) are anecdotal. A safe architecture avoids AccessibilityService entirely.
5. **Headway/Blinkist ad-SDK *activity*** — Exodus shows embedded tracker code, which is "ingredients," not observed network calls; actual data transmission depends on runtime behavior we did not capture.
6. **Khan Academy**: whether any sanctioned content/data surface (e.g., oEmbed, course JSON) exists today beyond public web pages — could not verify; treat API-less as the operating assumption.
7. **Does spaced repetition transfer from vocabulary to philosophy/prose?** SRS evidence is densest in fact-recall domains (§3.1); applying it to comprehension of classics is plausible but untested publicly.
8. **Communication-skills practice formats** have strong practitioner models but no app-format RCTs (§3.4) — the most under-evidenced part of the roadmap.
9. **"1% better" habit-tracking evidence** — the specific habit-formation meta-analyses could not be retrieved/verified this session (search instability); the goal-monitoring meta-analysis (Harkin 2016) is the verified adjacent finding. Treat the compounding "37×" math as motivational rhetoric (§3.5) and habit-loop specifics as design heuristics, not validated claims.
